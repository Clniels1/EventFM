What it is

An Acknowledgement of Country is a short statement at the start of an event (or on an event page) that recognises the Traditional Owners or Custodians of the land you're on and their ongoing connection to it. Anyone can give one.

It matters for reconciliation because it recognises Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples as the First Peoples of this land, and acknowledges the dispossession that followed colonisation. That only works if it's done sincerely, rather than treated as a box-ticking exercise.

Welcome vs Acknowledgement

Aspect Welcome to Country Acknowledgement of Country
Who gives it Traditional Owners of that Country, or people they've authorised Anyone
What happens Visitors are formally welcomed onto Country. Can include a speech, song, dance or smoking ceremony. A spoken or written statement of respect
Organising it Book through the local Land Council or Traditional Owner organisation, well ahead. Expect to pay a fee. Write it yourself after researching whose Country you're on

Writing your own

There's no set wording. In her TEDxSydney talk, Shelley Reys suggests making it personal and relevant to the occasion. The enhanced option on the event form covers:

  1. Who the Traditional Custodians are
  2. Respect to Elders past and present
  3. Why this Country matters to your event, in your own words
  4. Anything your event is actually doing, like booking First Nations artists

The generic option uses a fixed statement: "{{ generic_statement }}"
